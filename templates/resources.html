Resources

This page shows the kind of domain knowledge a real search-network app has to be built on — the alert types law enforcement actually uses, the hotlines a family needs in the first hour, and the start of a state-by-state directory. Phase 1 is a prototype; the structure below is exactly how this grows into a full 50-state resource system in Phase 2.

Missing-Persons Alert Color Glossary

When law enforcement issues a missing-persons alert, the color name tells first responders, media, and the public exactly what kind of case they're looking at — and how fast it needs to move. Any app built for this space needs to speak this language fluently.

AMBER

An abducted or endangered child.

SILVER

A missing older adult, or someone with a dementia-related impairment.

BLUE

A suspect sought after serious harm to a law enforcement officer.

PURPLE

A missing adult with certain disabilities, believed to be in immediate danger.

CAMO

A missing current or former military member with mental health concerns.

CLEAR / Endangered Missing Persons

A missing adult believed to be in danger, or a missing person with intellectual or developmental disabilities.

Criteria vary by state — some states use CLEAR, others fold this into their own endangered-adult alert. A production build needs a state-by-state lookup table, not one national rule.

National Hotlines

Verified against each organization's own published contact information. Numbers a family or a volunteer needs to have memorized.

Emergency — Immediate Danger

911

Call immediately. You do not need to wait 24 hours to report someone missing — that's a myth.

NCMEC — Missing & Exploited Children

1-800-THE-LOST

(1-800-843-5678) — 24/7

National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. Reports of a missing child, or child sexual exploitation.

National Human Trafficking Hotline

1-888-373-7888

Text 233733 — 24/7, 200+ languages

Confidential, live-answered. For suspected trafficking situations tied to a missing-persons case.

National Runaway Safeline

1-800-RUNAWAY

(1-800-786-2929)

Support and resources for runaway and at-risk youth, and the families looking for them.

NamUs — National Missing & Unidentified Persons System

namus.gov

The Department of Justice's free national case database. Families can submit case information and DNA samples directly. NamUs works through its case-management system and web intake rather than a public call-in line — the app can pre-fill a NamUs submission for a family (see the Phase 2 proposal).

StrongHearts Native Helpline

1-844-7NATIVE

(1-844-762-8483) — 24/7, confidential & anonymous

Built specifically for Native American and Alaska Native survivors of domestic and sexual violence — the population at the center of the Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women (MMIW) crisis. This line matters here specifically: it's severely under-resourced relative to the scale of the problem, and a search-network app that ignores it is ignoring the communities hit hardest.
